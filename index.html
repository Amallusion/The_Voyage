Every boat on the water is a sailor who has boarded.

Every worthwhile voyage begins with one decision: to take part.

For generations, Gulf pearling crews sailed out to the oyster banks with no certainty of what they would bring home. Every season asked for courage, preparation and a crew that relied on one another: divers below, haulers above, and a captain reading the sea.

The Voyage carries that spirit to KFUPM students who take on international competitions for their university and their country. The ship is the courage to leave familiar water. The pearl is what effort earns.

Five pillars, one string

Three voyages. None began with a guarantee.

1915

Fortitudine Vincimus

Endurance, Weddell Sea, Antarctica

The pack ice closed around Shackleton’s ship Endurance and crushed her. The mission was lost, yet after months on the ice and an open-boat crossing of the Southern Ocean, all 28 men came home.

For the shipLosing the ship is not losing the voyage.
1981

Sohar

Sur, Oman to Canton, China

Omani shipwrights built a ninth-century-style dhow without a single nail, its planks sewn with coconut-fibre rope. She sailed about 9,600 km, lay becalmed for almost a month, lost her mainsail spar to a squall, and reached Canton in July 1981. Today she stands on a roundabout in Muscat.

For the shipA ship on land can still carry the whole voyage.
1925

Sanat al-Tabbah

The pearl banks off Qatif, Bahrain and Qatar

In 1344 AH a sudden storm swept the pearl banks. Thousands of divers and haulers were lost in a single night, and the Gulf coast still remembers it as the Year of the Sinking. In the seasons that followed, the boats went back to the banks.

For the shipCourage is going back.

The second season

An original story written for The Voyage, not a historical account.

Salem was sixteen when the storm took his uncle’s boat. Every man reached the shore alive, but the boat, the season and every pearl they had gathered were gone. In the village they said the sea had given its answer.

His uncle said nothing. For weeks he walked the beach at dawn, carrying back every plank the sea returned. Through the winter the crew sat around the broken hull and sewed it together again, rope by rope, with their own hands.

When the next season opened, theirs was the first boat to leave. On the last day of the season, Salem made one dive more than anyone asked of him. He came up with the largest pearl the crew had ever seen.

His uncle never sold it. He set it into the bow, so the boat would always remember that it had sailed twice.
